Overview

System Design Arcade is a mobile app that teaches system design concepts through interactive, arcade-style games. This policy explains what data we collect, why, and how we handle it.

The short version: We collect your email and game progress if you sign in, and we measure how the app is used so we can improve it. We don't sell your data, we don't run ads, and we don't build advertising profiles.

What Data We Collect

If you play as a Guest:

  • We do not collect your name, email, or any other information that identifies you personally.
  • Game progress is stored only on your device (locally, via AsyncStorage).
  • We do collect anonymous usage analytics — see Usage Analytics below. These are tied to a randomly generated device identifier, not to you.

If you sign in (Google, GitHub, Apple, or email):

  • Email address — provided by your OAuth provider. Used to identify your account, and associated with your usage analytics.
  • Display name — if provided by your OAuth provider. You can change it in Settings.
  • Avatar — an emoji you pick in the app. If your Google or Apple account supplies a profile picture, we may store the link to it. We never upload or host photos from your device.
  • Game progress — scores, stars, streaks, and level completion data, synced to the cloud so you can access it across devices.
  • Country — a two-letter country code derived from your device's region setting, used for the country leaderboard.
  • Friends — if you add friends via a friend code, we store that connection.
  • Event participation — if you register for a timed event, we store your registration, when you registered, and your score at the event's start.

We do not collect your location (beyond the country code above), contacts, photos, microphone input, browsing history, or advertising identifiers.

Usage Analytics

We use PostHog for product analytics, so we can see which parts of the app work and which don't. This applies to both Guest and signed-in users.

What we measure:

  • Screens you open and the order you move between them.
  • Games and levels started, completed, or abandoned; scores and streaks.
  • Whether a challenge answer was correct, and what type of challenge it was. We do not record the content of your answers in analytics — only correct/incorrect and the challenge type. What you send to the optional cloud AI Coach is separate from analytics; see below.
  • Which features you tap (share, leaderboard, hints, daily challenge, theme settings, and similar).
  • App version and build, operating system and version, device type, and language.
  • Your IP address, which PostHog receives with each event and uses to derive an approximate region.

If you are signed in, these events are linked to your account and email. If you are a Guest, they are linked only to a random device identifier.

We do not use session recording, screen recording, or heatmaps — these are switched off. We do not use analytics for advertising, and we do not share or sell this data.

Analytics events are retained for 12 months. There is currently no in-app switch to turn usage analytics off — email us if you would like your analytics data deleted.

Error and Diagnostic Reports

When the app hits an error, a diagnostic report is sent to PostHog so we can fix it. A report contains the error type (for example TypeError), the error message, and a stack trace showing where in our code it happened. It also carries the same app version, device and session information as our other analytics events, and — if you are signed in — is associated with your account.

Error messages can occasionally contain incidental data such as an account identifier or a file path. We make a best effort to keep personal data out of these reports and are actively improving how they are filtered before they leave your device.

We do not collect crash reports from the operating system itself.

AI Coach (cloud; optional, off by default)

The app has an optional cloud AI Coach. It is switched off unless you turn it on (Settings → AI Coach (cloud)), needs you to be signed in, and is limited to a set number of uses in any 24 hours. When it is on, it is used in two places:

  • Design feedback. After you submit an architecture in a design challenge, it writes feedback on it.
  • "Ask the coach" questions, on phones without built-in AI only. After you answer a challenge you can ask a follow-up question about it. On phones with built-in AI the question is answered on the phone instead and never sent to us (see below).

Each time it is used, the following is sent to our AI coaching service — a Cloudflare Worker we operate, which passes the request to Groq (running Meta's Llama models):

  • For design feedback: the architecture you built, including the component names you typed, and the challenge brief and how your answer scored against it.
  • For a question: the question — one you picked from the app's list, or the words you typed — which challenge it is about (an identifier; our server looks up the challenge text itself), and the app's own notes on what was wrong with your answer, if anything was (for example "Missing: Cache").
  • Before anything leaves your device, what you typed is shortened and stripped of email addresses, links and most web addresses, phone numbers, long number sequences and @handles. Other things you type, such as a person's name, are not removed, so please don't type personal details into the coach.
  • A token proving you are signed in. Our server checks it and uses the account id inside it only to count your daily requests (see below). The token itself is not stored.

What is kept: our Worker does not log or store what you send. To enforce the daily limits it keeps a request count and the time the current 24-hour period started, filed under a code derived from your account id rather than the id itself (pseudonymous: we could work out which account it belongs to), and deletes it a day after your last request. It also keeps a count of all requests from everyone, which is not linked to you, and uses your IP address briefly (about a minute) to limit bursts of requests. We have turned on Groq's Zero Data Retention setting for the coach's requests, so Groq does not retain what the coach sends it or the answers it gives, including for troubleshooting or abuse monitoring, except where the law requires it to. Groq keeps only usage metadata, such as request counts, which contains no questions, designs or answers. See the Groq data policy.

Our usage analytics record that a question was asked, which kind (one from the list, or your own), the challenge type, which coach answered (on the phone or in the cloud), the outcome or an error code if it failed, and, if you tap "Report this answer", that you reported it and which challenge it was about. They never include the question or the answer.

Nothing is sent to this service unless you have switched the AI Coach on. You can turn it off again at any time in Settings.

On-device AI: "Explain it differently" and "Ask the coach"

After a wrong or partly wrong answer, some phones offer a button that rewords the explanation. The rewording is done by a language model that runs on your phone: Apple's on-device model on iPhone (Apple Intelligence), and Google's Gemini Nano on supported Android phones. The model is given the explanation and, for architecture challenges, the app's own notes on what was wrong with your answer (for example a missing part, or a connection drawn the wrong way). That text is not sent to us, to Apple or to Google (on Android, Google learns only how long it was; see below), and the reworded text is not stored.

"Ask the coach" works the same way on those phones. After you answer a challenge you can ask a follow-up question about it, picked from a list or typed. The on-device model is given your question, the challenge, its correct answer and explanation, and the app's notes on your answer if it was wrong. None of it is sent to us, to Apple or to Google (on Android, Google learns only how long it was; see below), and the answer is not stored. On phones without built-in AI, the coach is only available through the optional cloud AI Coach above.

Our usage analytics record that you tapped the button, which kind of model was used, whether the app had notes on your answer to give it, and whether it produced a rewording (or an error code if it failed). For "Ask the coach" they record the kind of question (one from the list, or your own), the challenge type, which kind of model answered, the outcome or an error code if it failed, and — if you tap "Report this answer" — that you reported it and for which challenge. On Android they also record when you start a model download from Settings and whether it finished or failed. They also record when you turn Settings → AI Features on or off. They never include any of the text.

On Android, the model is provided through Google's ML Kit, and ML Kit sends Google its own diagnostics and usage statistics. These include your device's manufacturer, model, OS version and build, and its ML hardware; our app's package name and version; identifiers, including a per-installation identifier that Google says is not meant to identify you or your phone; performance figures such as how long a request took; the size of the input and output (how long the text was, never the text itself); the feature version and settings; event types (such as starting the feature or downloading the model); error codes; and the configured language. Google uses this for diagnostics and usage analytics, sends it over HTTPS, and states that it does not pass it to third parties. See the ML Kit data disclosure.

This happens on Android 8 and later phones, whether or not they support Gemini Nano, even if you never tap the button: the app asks ML Kit whether the model is ready when Settings is open or the app returns to it, and after you answer a challenge, and ML Kit can report those checks. Turn off Settings → AI Features and, from then on, no AI feature appears and the app makes no requests to ML Kit. A model download the phone's system service has already started may still finish. Statistics ML Kit collected before you turned it off are handled by Google under its own terms.

If Gemini Nano is not yet on your Android phone, Settings offers to download it. The download only starts when you tap it and confirm, and it comes from Google through the phone's own system service.

Leaderboards and Social Features

System Design Arcade has public leaderboards. If you are signed in and appear on one, the following is publicly visible — including to people who are not signed in:

  • Your display name
  • Your total score and rank
  • Your country, on the country leaderboard

Your email address is never shown on a leaderboard.

You can remove yourself from public leaderboards at any time: Settings → Privacy → "Show me on leaderboard". You will still appear on the friends leaderboard of people who have added you as a friend. Guests do not appear on any leaderboard.

Notifications

If you turn on study reminders, the app schedules notifications locally on your device. We do not use a push notification service and we do not collect a push token.

How We Use Your Data

  • To authenticate your account and sync your game progress across devices.
  • To rank you on leaderboards, if you have not opted out.
  • To understand how the app is used, so we can improve the content and fix what's broken.
  • To diagnose and fix errors and crashes.

We do not sell your data, share it with data brokers, use it for advertising, or use it to build profiles for any purpose other than improving this app.

Data Storage & Security

  • Guest game progress is stored locally on your device only.
  • Signed-in user data is stored in Supabase (hosted on AWS) with row-level security enabled.
  • Analytics and diagnostic events are stored by PostHog in the United States.
  • Authentication tokens are stored securely on your device.
  • All communication with our servers uses HTTPS encryption.

Data Retention

Account data is retained as long as your account exists.

If you delete your account, your profile, game progress, statistics and event registrations are permanently deleted from our database, and your login is removed. Two things deliberately survive:

  • A minimal deletion record — your user ID, email address, IP address, browser/app user agent, and the time of deletion. We keep this so we can answer disputes about whether and when an account was deleted.
  • Analytics and diagnostic events already recorded, which expire on their normal 12-month schedule. Email us if you want them removed sooner.

Analytics events are retained for 12 months from when they were recorded. The user profile PostHog builds from them persists until you ask us to remove it.

Your Rights

You can:

  • Play without an account — Guest mode collects no personally identifying information.
  • Delete your account in the app — Settings → Data → Delete Account. This permanently removes your account and all associated server-side data.
  • Request data deletion by email — if you'd rather not use the in-app flow, email us and we will delete your account and all associated data within 30 days.
  • Opt out of public leaderboards — Settings → Privacy.
  • Turn off every AI feature — Settings → AI Features. With it off, no AI feature appears, nothing is sent to our AI coaching service, and on Android the app makes no further requests to Google's ML Kit. It is on by default, and applies to the phone it is set on.
  • Turn off the cloud AI Coach — Settings → AI Coach (cloud), shown while AI Features is on. Turning AI Features off also stops it; turning AI Features back on restores your earlier AI Coach choice. It is off by default.
  • Sign out — at any time from Settings within the app.
