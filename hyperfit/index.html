What's in the build

Strength & nutrition tracking, designed like Strava and Robinhood had a kid.

Anthracite base, strava-orange for the active set, robinhood-green when your session volume beats the last one. Mono numerics with tabular figures so every readout lines up. Every screen is built around one question: did the work happen, yes or no?

— 01
Anatomy-aware muscle heatmap
Recruitment-weighted volume per muscle, rendered onto a front/back silhouette. Toggle between volume mode and a recovery-decay model that shows what's actually fresh today.
— 02
Plan creator + active-plan rail
Build a multi-day split, assign sessions to days of the week, and the app schedules from there. One plan active at a time — no clutter.
— 03
Shareable workout cards
Every finished session generates a 1080×1350 card with your silhouette, top muscles, and PR badges — ready for stories. Re-share past sessions from history too.
— 04
PR detection
Per-exercise weight PRs detected the moment you log them. The 🔥 chip on the post-session screen and share card means it actually happened.
— 05
Ghost-value last-session inputs
Set fields pre-fill with what you lifted last time as faded placeholder text. No tap-to-clone — just a target you can beat or match without re-typing.
— 06
Editable session history
Calendar deep-links into a session detail view with grouped sets per exercise, duration, total volume, and a share button that works months later. Tap the pencil to fix a typo’d weight or rep without re-logging.
— 07
Nutrition tracking
Per-day macro pills, meal entries with slot (breakfast / lunch / dinner / snack) and a free-text label like “Pre-workout.” Cheat-day toggle that doesn’t pretend the meal didn’t happen. The This Week card folds nutrition + lifting into one signal.
— 08
Water intake, the way you actually drink
One-tap CUP or BOTTLE presets plus a CUSTOM AMOUNT field. Stored in ml, displayed in ml or oz per your setting. Water is the rare metric where more is unambiguously better, so it gets robinhood-green every time you log a tap.
— 09
Social shareable plans & cards
Flip a plan to shareable to mint a code; anyone with it lands on a public preview page with the muscle silhouette already lit up by intensity. Workout cards drop straight into Instagram / Stories / iMessage at 1080×1350. Rotate or revoke a share code anytime.
— 10
Onboarding that respects your time
Three screens: name + units, goal (BUILD / CUT / MAINTAIN / JUST TRACK), and pick a starter plan. Forward-only, kill-safe, under sixty seconds before your first set.

— Why this exists

A short story about an honest mirror.

The first gym I ever walked into smelled like chalk and old rubber. I was nineteen, skinny in a way that wasn’t flattering, and I’d downloaded six different tracker apps in the parking lot before going inside. By the third set of the day I had abandoned all of them. One wanted me to rate my mood. One was begging me not to break my streak. One had a coach avatar named “Brent” who clapped at me every time I tapped a checkbox. I didn’t need Brent. I needed to know whether I’d gotten stronger than last Tuesday.

Years later, after a long detour through cricket, chess engines, and a brief obsession with options chains, I came back to the same problem with sharper tools. I had become the kind of engineer who builds for himself first and the kind of lifter who actually cared about hypertrophy distribution across muscle groups. So I started building HyperFit — the app I wished had been on my phone in that first parking lot.

The rule was simple from day one: no streaks, no Brent, no gamified glitter. Just an honest mirror. Anthracite base because gyms are dim and your phone shouldn’t blow your pupils out between sets. Strava-orange for the active lift, because that’s the language your eyes already speak. Robinhood-green only when the session volume actually beat the last one — the green is earned, never decorative.

Today the build does a lot. On the lifting side, it logs every set with ghost values from your last session pre-filled in the input as a target you can match or beat. It detects PRs the moment you tap save and stamps a 🔥 on the post-session card. It renders a recruitment-weighted muscle heatmap on a body silhouette that shows what you’ve actually trained, not what you said you were going to train. You can fix a typo’d weight from three months ago without losing the session.

On the fueling side, it runs a full nutrition vertical — macro pills, per-meal entries you can label freely (“Pre-workout,” “3 a.m. raid,” whatever), and a cheat-day toggle that doesn’t pretend the meal didn’t happen. Hydration gets its own first-class surface: one tap for a CUP, one tap for a BOTTLE, a custom field for everything else, all stored in ml and rendered in your unit. Water is the rare metric where more is unambiguously better, so it gets robinhood-green every single time. The This Week card folds nutrition and lifting into one honest signal.

And it’s social, in a way that doesn’t require yet another feed. Every finished session generates a 1080×1350 share card — silhouette, top exercises, PR badges — that drops straight into Instagram, Stories, or iMessage and looks like a trophy, not a screenshot. Plans are shareable too: flip a switch, mint a code, and anyone with the code lands on a public preview page where the muscle silhouette is already lit up by intensity before they pick up a barbell. Rotate or revoke the code anytime. You can hand the whole thing to a friend in a sixty-second onboarding flow.

The next chapter is already in motion. Apple sign-in is queued for the public App Store submission, and Android is around the corner once iOS stabilizes. Realtime sync between devices is on deck, so the iPad on the rack and the iPhone on the bench finally agree. A Form Coach computer-vision layer will sit next to the set log — never in front of it — for the lifts where technique is the whole game. And eventually, recovery and readiness signal from sleep and HRV will join the heatmap so the app can answer “train or rest” with data instead of a vibe.

But the spine doesn’t change. HyperFit will always answer one question first: did the work happen, yes or no. Everything else is interest on that principal.

— The Hyperfocused Engineer
